Samsone: A Family of Open Small Audio Language Models for On-Device Inference ⭐

📅 2026-09-18👥 Piotr Masztalski, Michał K. Grzeszczyk, Olaf Sikorski
The success of Large Audio Language Models has driven the development of massive multimodal networks exceeding billions of parameters. However, the demand for privacy-preserving, low-latency processing has shifted focus toward Small Audio Language Models (SALMs) capable of on-device execution. The authors introduce Samsone, a family of SALMs designed for edge computing. The core model, Samsone-134M, establishes a new state-of-the-art for its size class across multiple benchmarks. Scaling laws are explored via Samsone-99M and Samsone-356M. Despite their compact footprint, the Samsone family delivers performance competitive with models orders of magnitude larger. Trained on publicly available data, with training code, weights, mobile-optimized checkpoints, and an open-source Android app for real-time on-device inference released.

Rethinking Music Tokenization: A Semantic Codec toward High-Fidelity LLM Music Generation ⭐

📅 2026-09-18👥 Huakang Chen, Guobin Ma, Yuepeng Jiang, Dake Guo, Jingbin Hu, Hanke Xie, Wenhao Li, Lingxin Xiong, Jian Zhao, Zhonglin Jiang, Yong Chen, Lei Xie, Pengcheng Zhu
Discrete audio tokenization has become the critical interface between raw waveforms and autoregressive modeling in recent music generation. Existing reconstruction-oriented tokenizers often mix musical structure with fine acoustic details, producing high-entropy tokens that are hard to model, while semantics-guided alternatives designed for speech do not fit music well. The authors rethink music tokenization around a measurable notion of music semantic content grounded in Music Information Retrieval tasks, and propose MuSeC, a music semantic codec that factorizes semantic and acoustic content directly from mixed signals without source separation. MuSeC preserves information required for high-fidelity reconstruction while producing more LM-friendly discrete units, improving reconstruction quality and yielding more predictable token sequences. Demos at https://longwaytog0.github.io/MuSeC/.

Listen Before You Speak: Response Planning from Listener Facial Reactions for Conversational Speech Generation ⭐

📅 2026-09-18👥 Yunji Chu
Conversational speech depends on dialogue context and the listener's immediately preceding behavior. The authors propose ReACT-TTS, a two-stage framework that uses a one-second pre-response listener facial sequence to plan the next utterance's emotion and prosody before speech realization. On a strict dyadic MELD protocol, temporal conditioning yields higher mean macro-F1 and VAD concordance than text-only across ten seeds. Ablations show temporal modeling performs best among visual variants. In a contextual-appropriateness study with 20 speech researchers, 76% of judgments prefer Temporal. The predicted response style is connected to a Grad-TTS backbone for end-to-end speech realization. Source code at https://github.com/CYJ1/ReACT-TTS_public.

Towards Zero-Shot Attribution of Synthetic Speech via Audio-Text Contrastive Retrieval ⭐

📅 2026-09-18👥 Cristian-Teodor Neamtu, Serban Mihalache, Stefan Smeu, Dan Oneata, Horia Cucu, Dragos Burileanu
Audio deepfake forensics is moving beyond a simple real-or-fake verdict toward attribution: which system generated the audio clip? Most source-attribution methods are closed-set classification, unable to name generators absent from training. The authors instead frame attribution as cross-modal retrieval: each generator is described in natural language, and a clip is attributed by retrieving the closest description in a shared audio-text embedding space. A frozen Wav2Vec2-BERT audio encoder is coupled with a frozen E5 text encoder aligned through small trainable projection heads. On MLAAD v9 (140 TTS models, 51 languages) under leave-models-out cross-validation, it reaches a model-level MRR of 58.4% for unseen generators, and even on failure often matches systems sharing the true generator's vocoder or architecture.

HAMMER: Harmonic-Aware Parallel Context Modeling and Discriminator-Free Perceptual Optimization for Speech Enhancement ⭐

📅 2026-09-18👥 Shang-Fu Chen, Szu-Wei Fu, Sung-Feng Huang, Rong Chao, Wen-Huang Cheng, Yu Tsao
Recent speech enhancement systems combine self-attention and Mamba but usually treat them as sequence mixers without explicitly exploiting harmonic periodicity, a strong cue for preserving voiced speech under noise. The authors propose a harmonic-aware, discriminator-free speech enhancer with (i) a Time-Frequency Harmonic-aware Attention-Mamba (TF-HAM) block running self-attention and bidirectional Mamba in parallel along both spectrogram axes plus a speech-adapted autocorrelation feed-forward network, and (ii) Metric-explicit perceptual refinement (MEPR) combining differentiable PESQ and log-likelihood-ratio losses. On VoiceBank+DEMAND it achieves 3.69 PESQ and 4.41 COVL with only 2.39M parameters, matching or beating discriminator-based systems; inference-time perceptual contrast stretching further raises PESQ to 3.79 without retraining.

GenTraceBench: A Benchmark for Tracing Audio Deepfakes Across Pre- and Post-training Stages

📅 2026-09-18👥 Li Wang, Kunyu Feng, Wan Lin, Dekun Chen, Qinke Ni, Xueyao Zhang, Lei Wang, Jie Shi, Haizhou Li, Zhizheng Wu
Modern TTS systems are rarely deployed unchanged; they are adapted through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or preference optimization (DPO/GRPO). The authors present GenTraceBench, spanning five TTS architectures, 16 pre-/post-training variants, and 49,728 utterances, to study whether fingerprints learned from a foundation generator remain valid after adaptation. Under a train-on-foundation, test-on-adapted protocol, DPO and GRPO generally preserve fingerprints, while some SFT and pre-training-data changes cause substantial drift, with effect sizes varying across three forensic backbones. In W2V-BERT verification, multi-shot enrollment reduces EER for the SFT condition from 44.4% to 11.0%.

📅 2026-09-18👥 Steven Vander Eeckt, Hugo Van hamme
